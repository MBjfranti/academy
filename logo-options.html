Barley & Bronze — the mark

Everything that was drawn, at the sizes that actually decide it: 16px favicon, 24–34px masthead, and large. Light and dark side by side throughout, because several of these were fine in one and wrong in the other.

The finding. Every pictorial mark died the same death, and it is worth naming once. Below about 24px a picture stops being the thing it depicts and becomes the nearest icon the reader already knows. The tablet became a file. The ingot became a sparkle. The barley sign became a rewind button. And this is a type-led site with two painted image registers and no flat-vector register at all — a pictogram would have been a third visual language introduced solely to fill a 16px square.

So the answer is typographic, and the ampersand is the mark.

Chosen

light

Locked stack, ampersand as counterweightchosen

BARLEY and BRONZE are both six letters and both start with B, so they can be tracked to exactly the same width — 0.1183em and 0.1em in Eczar at wght 700, measured off the font rather than eyeballed. Two equal lines make a block, and a block is a shape, which is what a reader recognises when they are not reading. The ampersand at 1.9em in copper spans both lines and balances it: the one drawn form in the identity, doing the job the killed icons were being asked to do.

The favicon: the same ampersand, as outlineschosen

Eczar at wght 800 rather than the masthead’s 700, because the lighter cut thins out at 16px and this one holds. No background chip: a rounded rectangle spends a third of a 16px budget on its own border and turns the mark into an app icon. The fill is the site’s copper token, with the dark-theme value behind a prefers-color-scheme query inside the file, so the favicon tracks the browser chrome the way the page tracks the reader.

Rejected — typographic

light

What was there beforerejected

Three faults. The two lines are different widths, so the block has no silhouette and reads as a line of type that wrapped rather than as a mark. The ampersand opens line two, which is a position no wordmark puts one in — it reads as an orphan, not an accent. And at 0.82rem it carries less weight in its own header than the copper pill on the active tab, so the brand loses to a nav item.

Rejected — pictorial

light

The clay tablet with a barley ear incised in itrejected

Killed before this round, and the lesson generalised. A rounded rectangle with a corner knocked off is the universal glyph for “document”, and nothing you put inside it wins that argument.

ŠE, the cuneiform sign for barleyrejected

The best idea in the set and the clearest failure. The site already publishes this sign and glosses it “barley”, so it would have been evidence rather than decoration — but it is six thin wedges, which is mush at 16px, and what survives the shrink is three left chevrons: the glyph for “skip to start”.

A barley ear, awnedrejected

Wheat-ear marks belong to breweries and bakeries, and the long awns that would have made this barley rather than wheat are hairlines that disappear below 32px. What is left at 16px is a beetle.

An oxhide ingot — the copper the Bronze Age actually tradedrejected

Conceptually the best fit for the second half of the name: the literal currency of the metal trade, off the Uluburun wreck, and this site was once called The Oxhide Kitchen. Drawn honestly, with its four horned corners, it is a four-pointed star. In 2026 that is the AI sparkle, and there is no drawing your way out of it.

A Canaanite jar — what Yadinu counted for a livingrejected

The strongest pictogram here. It is the shipping container of the age, it is what the narrator says he did for a living, and at 24 and 32px it is unmistakable. But the handles that make it a jar vanish at 16px and leave an egg, and a two-handled urn reads funerary.

A single barleycorn — the grain that was also the smallest weightrejected

The idea a provisioning scribe would have liked: the še was both the crop and the unit it was weighed in. The shape is a leaf, or a flame, or a coffee bean. No amount of meaning survives that.

Reference sheet, not a shipped asset, and nothing here is imported by the app. The live mark is the .mark block in src/index.css and the <Link className="mark"> in src/components/Layout.jsx; the favicon is public/favicon.svg. The rejected pictograms are redrawn here from the working files and were never wired in. Eczar is loaded from public/fonts/ relative to this file, so it renders the same opened from disk or served.
