# Costume and setting: the Aegean, c. 1226 BC

Written before shooting the Pylos frames, because the default failure here is worse than in
Anatolia or Egypt. Ask an image model for "a Greek palace" and it returns fluted white marble,
Doric capitals, men in draped himations and women in belted chitons. **All of that is seven
hundred years too late.** Classical Greece has not happened. It will not happen for longer
than the whole of Egyptian dynastic history to this point.

This file is scoped to `region: 'aegean'` and to the Mycenaean palace world Anniwiya writes
from.

## The one thing to get right

**Nothing is white marble and nothing is classical.** A Mycenaean palace is painted plaster
over rubble and timber, in strong flat colour, with frescoes on the walls. It is closer to a
painted Bronze Age Near Eastern building than to anything in a textbook about Greece.

Its columns **taper downward**, narrower at the base than at the top, in the Minoan manner,
and they are painted. Pylos is the one Mycenaean site preserving the imprint of *fluted*
column shafts. They are wood, not stone.

## The throne room at Pylos

Anniwiya works beside this room and enters it through the service passage, so it recurs.

- A hall of roughly thirteen by eleven metres.
- A **circular hearth about four metres across** in the centre, coated in plaster and painted
  with spirals and geometric bands.
- **Four columns around the hearth**, carrying a second-storey gallery from which people
  watched what happened below.
- **Griffins flanking the throne** in fresco.
- Wall paintings of kilted men carrying objects, and of robed men and women leading a bull.
- A floor of beaten, compacted earth. Not stone, not tile.
- A channel cut in the floor for wine poured to the gods.

## Dress

**Men.** A wrapped kilt to the knee, or a boat-necked sewn tunic — a short chiton — running
anywhere from the thigh to the ankle. Woven patterned bands at the edges on anyone of rank.
Long hair. Bare feet or hide sandals. Working men strip to the kilt.

**Women.** An ankle-length gown, or the flounced tiered skirt seen in the processional
frescoes, woven in elaborate patterns. Bronze dress pins at the shoulder, spiral bracelets,
strung beads. Hair worked at length: locks, tresses, buns and curls together, and the
arrangement carries rank and age.

**Women in these frames are fully clothed.** The open bodice of Minoan and Mycenaean art is a
convention reserved for goddesses and priestesses in painting, and it is not reproduced here
under any framing.

## Objects

- **The kylix**, a stemmed drinking cup, is the article's whole subject: pale buff clay, a
  plain foot and stem, undecorated, made in the thousand for a feast and nested rim-to-foot
  in tall stacks.
- Fine ware is pale buff with dark painted decoration, never glazed and never white.
- **Bronze, never iron.** Skewers, pins, balance pans, blades.
- Storage is in **pithoi**, ceramic jars taller than a person, sunk or standing in rows in
  magazine rooms.
- Clay firedogs in pairs with bronze skewers laid across them, over coals.
- Records are on unbaked clay tablets, palm-sized, written and stored damp.

## The prompt block

Paste into `Primary request` when a group of Mycenaeans is named, and into `Period accuracy`
as written.

> Late Bronze Age Mycenaeans of about 1226 BC: men in wrapped knee-length kilts or
> boat-necked sewn tunics, long hair, bare feet or hide sandals; women in ankle-length woven
> gowns or tiered flounced skirts with patterned bands, hair elaborately dressed in coils and
> tresses, bronze pins and strung beads. All women fully clothed.

> Period accuracy: painted plaster over rubble and timber; downward-tapering painted wooden
> columns; frescoes in strong flat colour; beaten earth floors; pale buff pottery with dark
> paint; bronze rather than iron. NO white marble, NO Doric or Ionic capitals, NO fluted
> stone columns, NO classical Greek chiton or himation or toga, NO laurel wreaths, NO
> hoplite armour, NO temple façades, NO Roman anything.

## Anniwiya in frame

Her identity file is `public/img/writers/anniwiya/face.webp`. Two things make her legible at
forty pixels and both belong in every `Input image` line:

- **The long dark indigo-brown scarf**, wound at the head and thrown back over the **left**
  shoulder. It is an Anatolian way of wearing cloth that nobody at Pylos wears, which is the
  entire point of it.
- **The strung cockle shells at her throat with one blue glass bead among them.**

She wears a well-made ankle-length saffron-gold wool gown with madder and dark blue woven
bands, a wide madder girdle wound twice, bronze pins, a spiral bracelet. **She is never
dressed as a peasant.** She was born in a king's house and now weighs and seals export oil,
and she stands like it: chin level, shoulders back, entirely still.
