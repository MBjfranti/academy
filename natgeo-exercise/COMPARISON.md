# Marking my own paper

Written after reading three real National Geographic pieces on the three briefs, and after
the drafts were finished and measured. The pre-registration is in
`docs/natgeo-preregistration.md`; nothing in it has been edited since.

| Brief | Real article | Words |

| Sharks | "How Cape Cod is adapting after its first fatal shark attack in 82 years" | ~3,200 |

## The one that matters: I deleted the reporter

**Prediction P4 was the exact inverse of the truth**, and it is the biggest single error in
the exercise.

I predicted the writer's first person would be sparse or absent, and I wrote all three
features at **0% first person**. Every real article has the reporter present and audible:

- Mosquitia: *"highly prominent throughout"* — Preston narrates the expedition himself:
  *"we would risk," "We awoke," "I felt."*
- Cape Cod: *"told me," "I spoke with," "I asked"* — used extensively.
- Tutankhamun: *"I notice that Zagloul signed his name next to Carter's and wonder if they
  conversed."*

This is not a stylistic preference I got backwards. It breaks a commitment I made in the
same document. **Commitment 3 says the access is part of the story** — the permits, the
season waited out, the difficulty of getting the material. You cannot report the difficulty
of access from nowhere. Preston can say the expedition was punishing because he was on it and
says so. By writing the narrator out, I removed the mechanism that makes the access
reportable, and then wrote assignment sheets congratulating myself on including it.

Worse, it was avoidable by inspection. My own drafts contain sentences like *"which is
somehow worse"* and *"briefly humiliating for everybody else present"* — a narrator is
plainly there, having reactions, while being grammatically forbidden from existing. I built a
voice that observes and judges but may not say "I", which is a stranger artefact than either
option.

**Diagnosis:** this is Barley & Bronze contamination that Rule 0 failed to catch. That site's
personas are the whole apparatus, so my defence against writer-centred prose is to erase the
writer. Transplanted here it produced a feature with no reporter in it.

## Where I over-delivered

Two of my predicted failures came out the opposite way, and both are cases where I wrote
*more* of something than the form actually uses.

**Mechanism.** I committed to "mechanism gets explained in full" and predicted a 100+ word
science passage. The Cape Cod piece has *minimal* biology — "heavily people and policy
centred." Mosquitia gives lidar about 300 words. Meanwhile I wrote long passages on
countercurrent heat exchange and the ampullae of Lorenzini, on cation exchange capacity in
terra preta, and on last-return filtering.

I have written **science features**. They write **narrative features with science in them**.
That is a real difference in kind and I am not sure the real answer is better — but it is the
difference, and I predicted it in the wrong direction.

**Unresolved disagreement.** I predicted I would be "too tidy" and resolve everything, and I
compensated by casting a deliberate unsettled fight into all three: Rizk vs Lindqvist on
erasure, Rele vs Kohl on the word, Céspedes vs Ferreira-Blake on population.

Mosquitia has *"minimal"* scepticism and "presents the findings affirmatively without academic
debate." The Cape Cod piece uses both "attack" and "bite" with **no discussion of terminology
at all** — the entire second spine of my shark feature is something the real article does not
do.

So my instinct was better than my prediction, and possibly better than the model. But it
means my drafts are more argumentative and more contested than the thing I was imitating.

## The smaller corrections

**Ages.** I studded three features with "Bobby Tavares, 63" and "Nora Aiken, 38". The real
articles give **occupation and institution**, and ages only occasionally. Name-comma-age is a
newspaper and *New Yorker* convention I imported wholesale. Mosquitia names thirteen people
with occupations and essentially no ages.

**Sentence length.** My target of 18–25 was too high. Real: 15–25, ~16–18, 15–20. My mixed
figures were 14.9 / 16.1 / 16.7 — **already correct**, and my narration-only figures of
21.0–21.6 are if anything slightly long. I spent the exercise "failing" a test I had
mis-specified, and my untutored instinct was closer than my stated theory.

**Subheads.** Present in all three, as predicted. But the Cape Cod piece uses short
all-caps signposts — RARE BUT INEVITABLE, WHAT TO DO?, RETURN OF THE SEALS, THE NEW NORMAL —
where mine are long descriptive clauses like "Why the works sit on the shingle and smell so
bad." Mine read as essay section titles. Theirs read as navigation.

**Datelines.** The Cape Cod piece opens "Wellfleet, Mass. —". I used none anywhere.

**Openings vary far more than I predicted.** Mosquitia opens on *"El Dorado. Atlantis. The
Lost City of Z."* — three fragments, no person, no action, and effectively a thesis about
legend. That is close to the opposite of my P5 rule. The Tut piece opens on a historical
scene in 1922, not a living person. Only Cape Cod matches my prediction, and it does so with a
memorial for a man who is already dead.

**Aphorism.** P11 said their density would be far lower than mine. The Tut feature ends:
*"The shadows move but the dark is never quite uplifted."* That is a landed beat, and it is
the last line of the piece. They do it too. They just do it once.

## Where the drafts hold up

**Length.** Mine came in at 3,139 / 3,690 / 3,156 — all inside the range, and the shark
feature is **longer than its real counterpart**. My loudest predicted failure, "too short,"
was wrong. Mosquitia at 5,500 is beyond anything I wrote, so the ceiling is real, but I was
not the disaster I forecast.

**Quote density and cast size** land where the real articles land.

**Angle on Tutankhamun.** The real centenary feature treats the Egyptian workmen as
*"largely absent"* — one passing reference to "a member of Carter's team whose name is lost
to history," no names, no discussion of the erasure. I made that erasure the spine of the
piece. Whatever else is wrong with my draft, I picked the more current and more contested
story, and I would defend that choice.

---

## An error I made and had to fix

I sited my invented bite survivor "off Newcomb Hollow" — which turned out, on checking, to be
the exact beach where a real 26-year-old was killed in 2018. Inventing an incident at the
location of a real death is careless irrespective of intent. The invented incident has been
moved to an invented beach. The real fatality stays in the piece as a dated fact and nothing
more.

## What I would change before writing a fourth

1. **Put the reporter in.** First person, used sparingly but audibly, and specifically to
   carry the access: the waiting, the permission, the flight that did not go.
2. **Cut most of the ages.** Keep occupation and institution.
3. **Cut the mechanism by about a third**, or accept that I am writing a science feature and
   stop calling it a Geographic pastiche.
4. **Shorten the subheads** to navigational signposts.
5. **Keep the unresolved disagreements.** They are not house style, and they are better.
6. **Retire P12.** It measured the wrong thing and the mixed figure is dragged down by quotes.
   The narration-only figure is the real test, and 18–22 is the honest target.
