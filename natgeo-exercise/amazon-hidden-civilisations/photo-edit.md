# Photo edit: The Country Under the Canopy

> **Specified, not shot.** Nothing generated. Invented subjects; see `README.md`.

Eleven frames. The controlling problem is that **this story is invisible**. The subject is
buried under a canopy, and every honest photograph of the ground shows a hill with trees on
it. That is not a failure to be worked around. It is the story, and the edit is built to
stage it: the reader is shown the unreadable thing first, then given the instrument, then
shown the same ground again.

The second decision: **no drone-over-rainforest sunrise.** It is the stock image of the
region, it says nothing, and it carries exactly the "pristine wilderness" framing the piece
spends 700 words dismantling.

---

## 1. OPENER — full-bleed spread

**Assignment.** Céspedes and two others standing on the platform, mid-conversation, in
closed forest. Flat green light. Absolutely nothing visible but trees, leaf litter and three
people on ground that reads as level.

**Technique.** Overcast midday, 35mm, straight documentary, no drama whatsoever. The frame
must be actively boring, and the printing should not rescue it.

**Job.** The reader has to fail to see it first. Everything after this frame depends on
having been shown nothing and told that nothing is a monument. Opening on the lidar image
would throw the whole edit away.

**Caption.** Paola Céspedes on the summit platform of a Casarabe settlement complex. "You are
standing on it," she says. "You have been standing on it for about ten minutes."

---

## 2. Facing page — the reveal

**Assignment.** The hillshaded bare-earth model of exactly the ground in frame 1, at matched
orientation, with the platform, moat and four causeways plain.

**Technique.** Screen capture from the survey data, printed full page, no annotation, no
arrows, no labels. Same compass orientation as frame 1, which requires shooting frame 1 to
match the model rather than the other way round.

**Job.** The turn of the whole story, executed as a page turn. Frames 1 and 2 must run as a
spread pair so the reader's eye does the work the instrument did.

**Caption.** The same ground, with the forest removed in software. Ranked platforms, a moat
on three sides, and causeways running straight into the savanna for kilometres.

---

## 3. Half page

**Assignment.** Reinholt in the aircraft, orange headset, laptop on his knees, the open port
in the floor beside him with the instrument bolted over it.

**Technique.** In-flight, wide, available light through the cabin windows, some vibration
blur accepted. Include the hole in the floor: readers do not know there is a hole in the
floor.

**Job.** Makes the instrument physical and slightly absurd. Also earns the mechanism passage
by showing that this is a machine in a small aeroplane, not a satellite.

**Caption.** The unit fires hundreds of thousands of pulses a second. In closed canopy, two
or three percent reach the ground. "Everything I do is throwing away 97 percent of the data,"
Reinholt says.

---

## 4. Small inset

**Assignment.** A raw point cloud on screen, zoomed to a slice, before filtering: an
unintelligible fog of dots.

**Technique.** Screen capture, small, deliberately illegible.

**Job.** Shows what the data looks like before interpretation, which stops the model in frame
2 reading as a photograph. The reader should understand that somebody decided what was
ground.

**Caption.** Raw returns before filtering. Distinguishing ground from vegetation is an
interpretive act, and it is where earthworks are found or lost.

---

## 5. Full page — environmental portrait

**Assignment.** Yuvané barefoot on the causeway at the point where he stopped, weight
forward, looking down at ground that reads as flat. Feet sharp and in frame.

**Technique.** 50mm, low, late afternoon side light so the fifteen-centimetre break in slope
throws the only shadow in the picture. The frame has to make visible the thing he found by
feel.

**Job.** The edit's argument about knowledge. He located a terrace edge the automatic
classification smoothed away. The photograph has to let the reader see it too, which means
the light is doing the same job the software failed to do.

**Caption.** Ignacio Yuvané found two terrace breaks the survey's classification had
discarded as noise. "You cannot feel it in boots," he says.

---

## 6. DOUBLE-PAGE SPREAD

**Assignment.** The soil pit wall: eighty centimetres of black terra preta above sharply
distinct red-yellow subsoil, with Marchetti's trowel and hand in the frame for scale, and
charcoal and pottery flecks visible in the black.

**Technique.** Tripod, careful flat lighting to hold detail in both the black and the red
without flash falloff, focus-stacked. The colour boundary must be honest — no grading that
exaggerates it.

**Job.** This is the frame that carries the piece's biggest single claim: the soil was made.
It is also the only image here where the evidence is legible without translation, and it runs
as the spread for that reason.

**Caption.** Amazonian dark earth: charcoal, bone, pottery and household waste accumulated
over centuries. Surrounding soils are exhausted after about three seasons. This is farmed
today with nothing added.

---

## 7. Small — specimen

**Assignment.** Charcoal fragment from the pit at high magnification, showing the intact
cell-wall pore structure.

**Technique.** Macro or low-magnification SEM. Focus-stacked.

**Job.** The mechanism, made visible. The text says the charcoal behaves like a charged
sponge; this shows the sponge.

**Caption.** Burnt wood retains its cellular structure as a rigid carbon skeleton, holding
nutrients and housing microbial communities for millennia.

---

## 8. Half page

**Assignment.** Kaxuyana in a Brazil nut stand, hand flat on a trunk, looking up into a
canopy of one species.

**Technique.** Vertical, wide, from low, so the repetition of the same tree fills the frame
and reads as a plantation rather than a forest.

**Job.** Argues that the forest is partly an artefact. The composition must make the
over-representation obvious, because the text asserts it and a photograph can demonstrate it.

**Caption.** Dozens of Amazonian tree species show long-term human selection, and cluster
near archaeological sites. "You are looking at a garden that has been left alone for five
hundred years."

---

## 9. Half page

**Assignment.** Ferreira-Blake at his desk with a printed journal figure annotated in red,
mid-annotation.

**Technique.** Tight, top-lit, the red pen marks legible enough to read as objections without
being readable as text.

**Job.** Gives the sceptic a face and a working method, so his section is not a rhetorical
foil. An edit that photographs one side of an unresolved argument has resolved it.

**Caption.** "I accept the earthworks. Every single one," Ferreira-Blake says. "What I dispute
is a number arrived at by multiplying a mound by an assumption."

---

## 10. Full page — the loss

**Assignment.** Arze's field: a levelled mound, tractor tracks across the flattened crown,
cattle on it, late light. Arze small in the frame or absent entirely.

**Technique.** Long lens from a distance, compressed, so the truncated profile of the mound
reads against the intact one behind it. Both in the same frame if the ground allows it.

**Job.** The only frame showing what is being lost while the argument runs. It must not be
prosecutorial — he told the reporter himself, and the caption says so.

**Caption.** Arze levelled two mounds with a rented tractor. "Nobody told me it was anything.
It is a hill in my field." An earthwork has no legal protection until it is recorded.

---

## 11. CLOSER — full page

**Assignment.** The causeway at dusk with Yuvané small and walking away along it, the raised
bank running dead straight to the treeline behind him.

**Technique.** Long lens, last light, compressed so the straightness of the bank is
undeniable across the whole frame. He is leaving the camera, and he is not looking back.

**Job.** Returns to the opening third and leaves it open. The causeway is doing its job in
the picture, which is the closing line's claim, so the image states it without the caption
having to.

**Caption.** The causeway holds the crown it was given about a thousand years ago, on ground
that floods every year and has flooded every year since.

---

## Rhythm

**Spread pair (1+2)**, half, inset, page, **spread**, small, half, half, page, page.

Two spreads. The first is the trick — invisible ground, then the same ground revealed — and
it runs at the front because everything else depends on the reader having been fooled once.
The second is the soil, because the soil is the harder and better idea.

## What the edit refuses

- **No drone sunrise over unbroken canopy.** It is the stock photograph of Amazonia and it
  asserts exactly the pristine-wilderness framing the story dismantles.
- **No reconstruction painting.** The temptation with vanished architecture is an artist's
  impression of the city at its height. It would be the most reproduced image in the package
  and the least evidenced, and it would settle the population argument by illustration.
- **No gold, no burial, no skull.** None of them is what this story is about.
- **No aerial of the causeway from directly overhead** — the model in frame 2 already does
  that better, and a real aerial would just look like a line in grass.
