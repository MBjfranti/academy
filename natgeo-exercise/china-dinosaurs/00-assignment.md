# Assignment: new dinosaur finds in China

> Imagined feature. All quoted speech and all named sources are invented characters. Real
> published findings, institutions and the 1999 Archaeoraptor affair appear as documented
> public record only. See `natgeo-exercise/README.md`.

## Peg

Two things. Fossil beds in Fujian have produced a bird-like theropod from a window of time
that was nearly blank in the record, roughly 150 million years ago, between *Archaeopteryx*
and the famous feathered animals of Liaoning. And the discipline is midway through a
generational argument about where its specimens come from, because the best ones in the world
were dug out of farmland by people paid by the piece.

## Nut graf *(paragraph 6-8)*

> China holds the best record of the origin of birds that exists anywhere, and it holds it
> because of a particular accident of geology: volcanic ash falling into still lake water,
> fast enough to bury an animal whole and fine enough to hold the impression of a feather.
> That record was assembled largely by farmers. For thirty years, palaeontologists have
> bought fossils from the people who found them, on the reasoning that a bought specimen is a
> saved specimen, and the market that reasoning created has produced some of the most
> important fossils ever described and at least one outright forgery that fooled a great many
> professionals. The animals coming out of the ground now are being excavated under rules
> written by people who watched that happen.

## Fact dossier (58 items; ~a third used)

`[soft]` marks anything I would verify.

**Geology and preservation**
1. The Jehol Biota, Liaoning and neighbouring provinces, roughly 130-120 million years old.
2. Yixian and Jiufotang Formations are the principal fossil-bearing units.
3. Preservation is exceptional because of fine volcanic ash falling into still, stratified lake water.
4. Anoxic lake bottoms prevented scavenging and slowed decay.
5. Fine ash preserves impressions at near-cellular scale.
6. Soft tissue, feathers, stomach contents and eggs are all preserved.
7. Fossils split along bedding planes, producing part and counterpart — two halves of one animal.
8. Part and counterpart can be sold separately, which is how specimens are destroyed as data.
9. The Zhenghe fauna in Fujian dates to roughly 148-150 million years **[soft]**.
10. That interval is close to *Archaeopteryx* and was poorly represented in the record.
11. *Fujianvenator prodigiosus* was described from Fujian in 2023, with unusually long lower legs.
12. Its limb proportions suggest a wading or running animal rather than a flying one **[soft]**.

**Feathers, birds, colour**
13. *Sinosauropteryx*, described 1996, was the first non-avian dinosaur found with filaments.
14. *Microraptor* has flight feathers on all four limbs.
15. *Anchiornis* has been reconstructed in colour from melanosome geometry.
16. Melanosomes are the organelles that hold melanin pigment.
17. The pigment itself does not survive; the organelle's shape does.
18. Eumelanosomes are elongate, sausage-shaped, and correspond to black and dark grey.
19. Phaeomelanosomes are spherical and correspond to reddish-brown.
20. The inference is calibrated against melanosome shape in living birds.
21. Critics argue diagenesis can alter melanosome shape during fossilisation **[soft]**.
22. Critics also argue some structures identified as melanosomes may be bacterial.
23. Birds are theropod dinosaurs; the question is not whether but how and when.

**The trade**
24. Many Liaoning fossils are found by farmers on agricultural land.
25. Payment is typically by the piece.
26. Chinese law restricts the export of vertebrate fossils.
27. Significant numbers have nonetheless left the country **[soft]**.
28. Some specimens have been repatriated after description **[soft]**.
29. Forgery is a recognised problem: composites assembled from multiple animals.
30. Fakes are often assembled from genuine fossil material, which defeats simple tests.
31. CT scanning reveals composites by showing discontinuous internal structure.
32. UV light reveals glue and filler used in composites.
33. In 1999 a specimen called "Archaeoraptor" was published in *National Geographic* as a
    missing link between dinosaurs and birds.
34. It was subsequently shown to be a composite of at least two animals.
35. The magazine retracted it. The affair is a standard cautionary case in the field.
36. Part of the specimen turned out to belong to a genuine and important animal,
    *Microraptor* **[soft]**.
37. The episode hardened peer-review norms about unprovenanced specimens.
38. Many journals now decline papers on specimens without documented provenance.
39. That policy protects the record and also excludes some genuine fossils permanently.

**Excavation and lab**
40. Modern practice records the exact bed, orientation and matrix of every specimen.
41. Provenance data is what allows dating, environmental reconstruction and association.
42. A fossil without provenance can be described anatomically and little else.
43. Preparation is done under a microscope with air scribes and needles.
44. A single block can take months to prepare.
45. Preparation is irreversible; a mistake removes evidence permanently.
46. Acid preparation is used for some matrices and is unforgiving.
47. CT scanning allows internal structure to be read without preparation.
48. Synchrotron imaging can resolve features at micron scale but beam time is scarce.

**People, place, sensory**
49. Fieldwork in Liaoning is seasonal; winter freezes the ground.
50. Quarries are often on or beside working farmland.
51. Farmers' knowledge of local stratigraphy is frequently detailed and unrecorded.
52. Split shale smells faintly of sulphur when freshly opened **[soft]**.
53. Fresh matrix changes colour as it dries, which is how experienced eyes spot fossil.
54. Provincial museums hold large uncatalogued backlogs **[soft]**.
55. Chinese palaeontology has grown from a small field to a global centre within one career.
56. Many major papers now have Chinese first authors and international co-authors.
57. Fieldwork permits involve provincial as well as national authorities.
58. Photography of unpublished specimens is routinely restricted.
