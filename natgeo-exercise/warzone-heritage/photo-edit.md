# Photo edit: The Shield Is a Map

> **Specified, not shot.** Nothing generated. Invented subjects and places; see `README.md`.

Ten frames, and the edit has a constraint no other in this exercise has: **the pictures are
themselves part of the risk the story is about.** An exterior that identifies the town, a
recognisable skyline, a road junction, a mine headframe, all of them do the job the Blue
Shield does. Every frame below is composed so that it cannot be used to find anything.

That is not a compromise on the photography. It is the subject, applied to the photography,
and the captions say so where it matters.

---

## 1. OPENER — full page

**Assignment.** The exercise book open on a desk, Krylenko's hands at the edge of frame, the
squared paper and four different handwritings visible. The shorthand destinations are
illegible at reproduction size.

**Technique.** Overhead copy-stand, available light, shot at a resolution and angle that
deliberately will not resolve the destination column. That degradation is chosen in camera
rather than applied afterwards, so no full-resolution file exists.

**Job.** Puts the entire security apparatus of a 3,140-object collection on one page: a
school notebook. Establishes immediately that this story runs on paper because the
alternative is worse.

**Caption.** The museum's working inventory since the ninth day of the war: 48 pages, four
hands, 3,140 lines. Destinations are written in a shorthand four people can read.

---

## 2. Full page

**Assignment.** The four Blue Shield emblems in the cupboard, still in their original
packaging, shelf edge and a box file visible.

**Technique.** Flat, frontal, unglamorous, on-camera fill. It should look like a stationery
cupboard, because it is one.

**Job.** The whole thesis in one object. Seventy years of international law, bought,
delivered, and never taken out of the wrapper.

**Caption.** Krylenko has had the emblems since 2015. The 1954 Hague Convention gives cultural
property a protective marking. She will not use it.

---

## 3. Half page

**Assignment.** A monument wrapped in scaffolding, geotextile and sand, in a location that
cannot be identified: framed tight so no street, skyline or signage enters the frame.

**Technique.** Long lens, compressed, shot from within a doorway. Overcast.

**Job.** Shows the cage-not-blanket construction the text describes, including the membrane
layer between sand and stone that is the part most often skipped.

**Caption.** Sandbagging stops fragmentation, not blast. The membrane between sand and stone
matters: sand held against limestone for two winters causes damage no shell came near.

---

## 4. DOUBLE-PAGE SPREAD

**Assignment.** The struck church, north elevation, in daylight. Wall scarred to four metres
and structurally sound; every window gone. Shot from inside the graveyard looking at the
damage, with the crater edge in the near foreground.

**Technique.** Tripod, mid-morning, 28mm, deep focus so crater, wall and window line all
hold. No people. Nothing beyond the churchyard wall in frame.

**Job.** This is the spine of the package and the reason the edit exists. Frame 3 explains
what sand does; this frame proves it in one elevation. The wall took fragments and survived.
The unprotected glass took overpressure and did not. A reader can verify the physics from the
picture.

**Caption.** The strike landed about 11 metres from the north wall. The masonry, which takes
fragmentation, is intact. The windows, which do not, are gone.

---

## 5. Small inset

**Assignment.** Savchenko's bare hands in rubble, close, no face.

**Technique.** Macro-ish, 50mm at minimum focus, natural light, shot from above his shoulder.

**Job.** The one image where the conservation story meets the ordnance story. He removes his
gloves to feel for wire, and the picture says why the two priorities collide without the text
having to argue it.

**Caption.** "Everybody tells me not to disturb the rubble," Savchenko says. "I still have to
walk through it."

---

## 6. Full page

**Assignment.** Ponomarenko's van interior at night, the disconnected dome light visible,
crates strapped down, the windscreen showing nothing but dark.

**Technique.** Long exposure on a beanbag, no flash, ambient only. Stationary vehicle.

**Job.** Makes the transport risk physical. The reader has been told a van does more damage
than a blast on most days; here is the van.

**Caption.** Ponomarenko has driven 34 runs, uninsured, with the interior light disconnected
so that opening the door at night shows nothing.

---

## 7. Half page — the pair with frame 8

**Assignment.** A panel painting face-up in an open crate, packing visible, immobilised
inside the case rather than the case being cushioned.

**Technique.** Overhead, soft raking light to show the panel's surface without specular
glare.

**Job.** Shows what "immobilised in the crate" actually means, which is the detail that
decides whether an evacuation works.

**Caption.** Nineteen panel paintings could not travel. A wooden support and its paint layer
respond to humidity at different rates, and the resulting damage appears across the year
after the journey, not during it.

---

## 8. Half page

**Assignment.** The east cellar: bare, dry, cold, 19 crated panels stacked on battens clear
of the floor, a thermometer and hygrometer taped to the wall and legible.

**Technique.** Tripod, long exposure, work light only. The instrument readings must be
readable in print.

**Job.** Runs against frame 7 and closes the argument. Also the only frame that shows a guard
was right and a survey was wrong, since this is the cellar the survey rated equal to the wet
one.

**Caption.** The east cellar stays dry because of a drain that appears on no plan the museum
holds. The site survey rated both cellars comparable. The guard knew better.

---

## 9. Full page

**Assignment.** Father Dovhan in the empty church, standing at the icon screen where eleven
icons are absent, the fixings still in place.

**Technique.** 35mm, from behind and to one side, window light through the destroyed
openings. He is small in the frame.

**Job.** The human cost of a decision that was correct. The icons survived because they left
over his objection, and he has said he was wrong, and the frame should not gloat.

**Caption.** Eleven icons left this church eleven months before the strike. "They were
painted to be looked at," Dovhan says. "In a box, it is only wood."

---

## 10. CLOSER — full page

**Assignment.** Krylenko walking away up the yard at dusk, bag over her shoulder, building
soft behind her, nothing identifiable in frame.

**Technique.** Long lens, last light, from the top of the yard. She is leaving and does not
look back.

**Job.** Returns to the opening subject. The book is in the bag, which is where it stays,
and the frame ends on the fact rather than on a flourish.

**Caption.** The only complete account of where 3,140 objects went travels in that bag, and
has done for two years.

---

## Rhythm

Page, page, half, **spread**, inset, page, half, half, page, page.

One spread, at frame 4, on the damaged building. Everything before it is preparation and
everything after it is consequence.

## What the edit refuses, and why it matters more here

- **No identifiable exteriors, skylines, junctions or signage.** Agreed in advance as a
  condition of access, and it is also simply correct: a photograph that locates a store is
  the same object as a marked emblem.
- **No aerial or drone work of any kind.**
- **No image of the mine, its headframe, its road or its name.**
- **No human remains, no graves in a state that identifies a burial, no wounded.**
- **No triumphal "treasure saved" image.** Gold in a crate under a work light is the picture
  every desk wants, it flatters everyone involved, and it is the opposite of what these
  people spend their days doing, which is choosing between bad options in the cold.
