# Evidence spine

- Egyptian daily temple ritual texts and offering lists.
- The reversion and redistribution of offerings to temple staff.
- Temple estates, workshops, and personnel that supplied cult meals.
- Private tomb chapels, funerary endowments, and family offerings to the dead.
- The ka-priest, employed to present offerings and speak the name of a dead man, and
  remunerated from the reverted offerings themselves rather than separately.
- Mortuary endowments of land or income intended to fund a cult in perpetuity, and the
  observed tendency of private cults to lapse within a generation or two as endowment and
  family memory both fail.
- Appeals to the living: inscriptions placed at tomb chapels asking any literate passer-by to
  speak the name of the dead and pour water.
- Fowl, bread, beer, meat, wine, honey, and coriander in Egyptian food evidence.

## Declared reconstruction

Amenmose, his arguments with Henut, and the specific chapels on her hillside are invented.
His pay arrangement and his prediction about lapsed cults are not.

Henut’s kitchen procedure, the minor noble’s household, and her visit to her husband’s chapel are narrative reconstructions. No surviving source gives this duck recipe as a continuous method.
