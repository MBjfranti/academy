# Evidence spine

- Mycenaean plain stemmed cups and large drinking-vessel deposits at palace sites.
- The Pylos pantries, which held some 6,593 vessels, about 77 per cent of the palace's entire
  ceramic assemblage. One pantry held over five hundred kylikes; a single wooden crate in
  another held one hundred and sixty-eight. Nineteen distinct kylix types are recorded, and
  the kylix accounts for close to half of all the pottery at the site.
- Feasting as a political instrument rather than a meal: commensality that mobilised labour,
  created alliances and stated hierarchy, with the vessel issued to a guest marking their
  inclusion.
- Miniature kylikes recovered with burnt animal bone, indicating the same drinking gesture
  performed as offering.
- The palace and its feasting deposits belong to Late Helladic IIIB, roughly 1320 to 1190 BC,
  so this article sits inside the period the evidence describes.
- Pylos Linear B allocations of livestock, grain, wine, oil, honey, and spices.
- PY Un 2, recording provisions issued for a ceremonial occasion connected with the
  initiation of the wanax: barley in bulk, flour, olives, honey, figs, one ox, twenty-six
  rams and six ewes, two he-goats and two she-goats, one fattened pig and six sows, and wine.
  The list Anniwiya reads over a scribe's shoulder is this allocation, given in her own units
  and without its tablet number.
- Linear B oil allocations distinguishing lamp, culinary, unguent and offering uses.
- The Pylos megaron, central hearth, libation channel, and painted interior.
- Ceramic firedogs and bronze skewers as evidence for hearth cooking.

## Voice boundary

Anniwiya cannot cite excavation room numbers, vessel totals or period labels. She knows the
store because she works in it: the crate of a hundred and sixty-eight, the nineteen shapes,
and the fact that the cup outnumbers everything else are hers by handling rather than by
reading.

## Declared reconstruction

Anniwiya cannot cite a tablet number or the term Linear B. She meets the allocation as a
document a scribe left face up, and reads it the way a storekeeper reads a list.

Alektryon, his carts, the visiting captain, the feast, and every exchange with Anniwiya are invented. The article uses the cups and allocations to reconstruct the scale and social order of palace feasting. Anniwiya’s movement between royal and working spaces belongs to her declared fictional biography.
