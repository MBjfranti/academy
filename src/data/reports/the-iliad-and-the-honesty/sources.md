# The Iliad and the Honesty

## Evidence spine

- *Iliad*, especially Books 1, 6, 9, 11 and 24, treated as evidence for a later form of an oral tradition. Book 1 links honour to the captive prize; Book 6 sets Hector’s pursuit of *kleos* against Andromache’s clear account of the city’s fall and her coming enslavement.
- Pylos Linear B ration series concerning women and children identified by place of origin.
- Hittite diplomatic texts concerning Wilusa and Millawanda.
- Archaeology of Late Bronze Age Troy and the western Anatolian coast.
- Scholarship on oral-formulaic composition, performance, and the chronology of the Homeric poems.

## Checked personnel evidence

- PY Ab 573 records sixteen *mi-ra-ti-ja* women, three girls and seven boys at Pylos, with grain and fig rations. The related Aa, Ab and Ad series connect groups of women and children across counts, rations and family relationships.
- The Pylos personnel tablets classify groups by occupation or place of origin. Attested external labels include women associated with Miletos, Knidos, Lemnos, Chios, Kythera, Halikarnassos and Aswia.
- The National Archaeological Museum identifies more than six hundred children in the Pylos palace workforce. Groups containing younger children occur with adult women; older boys also occur with groups of men.
- Thomas Palaima notes that scholars have commonly explained the foreign women through slave purchase or raiding. One group, *ra-wi-ja-ja*, carries a designation translated as captives or women acquired through plunder. The records do not establish the route by which every origin-labelled group reached Pylos.

## Claims requiring a source check before publication

- The precise tablet number and quantities used for the Milesian women.
- The safest wording for the relationship between Wilusa and Troy.
- The defensible duration and season of a typical Aegean coastal raid.
- The independent Bronze Age evidence for every component of the kykeon.
- The defensibility of placing an ancestral song with these episodes at Pylos in 1226 BC.

## Invented material

Anniwiya, her marriage, captivity and personal memories are invented. They are built to fit
the administrative category represented by Milesian women in the Pylos archive. The singer,
his performance, the audience’s familiarity, and the claimed age of the song are also
invented. No surviving evidence places this performance at Pylos in 1226 BC.
