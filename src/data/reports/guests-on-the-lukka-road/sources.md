# Evidence spine

- Hittite instructions to frontier post governors (the BĒL MADGALTI material) covering provisioning, road maintenance, garrison duties, and the obligations of local communities toward the crown.
- Administrative and treaty material recording supply owed to passing troops, and the counting
  of that supply.
- BĒL MADGALTI, literally "lord of the watchtower", the title of an official governing a
  frontier or rural district. His duties in the instructions run across fortifications and
  garrisons, cisterns and water supply, roads, the hearing of disputes, the correct
  observance of festivals, and the extraction of staple goods and labour from the district.
- The instructions belong to a group of roughly twenty Hittite administrative texts issued to
  officials, and are associated chiefly with the reigns of Tudhaliya I and Arnuwanda I,
  earlier than this article's setting. Yadinu meets the office rather than the tablet.
- The Lukka lands as territory repeatedly claimed, campaigned in, and never securely held; western and southern control exercised through sworn treaty, tribute, and hostage rather than occupation.
- Hittite military movement dominated by small detachments on established road networks, with large field armies as the exception rather than the pattern.
- Sheep as the ordinary slaughter animal, and the immediate consumption of liver, heart, and kidneys because offal will not keep.
- *Kariya* as a Hittite preparation of grilled liver and heart, attested by name in festival and cult inventories without a stated method.

## Declared reconstruction

The town, Ḫuzziya, the sixty men, the four donkeys, and every quantity in the article are invented. The eleven-day march and the three-days-carried figure are derived from the physical limits of pack transport rather than from a surviving itinerary.

The article's central claim — that the size of a column is set by what the road can supply, and that the practical edge of Hittite authority is the last place still meeting its obligation — is an argument built from the provisioning material. No single text states it in those terms.
