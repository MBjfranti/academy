# Evidence spine

- The reign of Ramesses II through 1226 BC, his fifty-fourth regnal year under the conventional chronology.
- The repeated royal jubilees already celebrated by that point in his reign.
- Deir el-Medina ostraca recording rations, attendance, illness, household business, and disputes.
- Tomb-worker schedules and the village’s dependence on deliveries from the Nile valley.
- Egyptian bakery scenes, conical bread moulds, and surviving emmer loaves.

## Chronology rule

The article exists in 1226 BC. Ramesses II is alive. His death, the accession of Merenptah, and the ration strikes under Ramesses III remain in the future and cannot enter Henut’s knowledge.

## Declared reconstruction

Henut’s apprenticeship, family, Meret, and their dialogue are invented.
