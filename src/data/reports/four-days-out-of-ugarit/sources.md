# Evidence spine

- Old Babylonian and Late Bronze Age merchant letters concerning pack loads, tolls, losses, routes, and safe conduct.
- Donkey caravans, water stops, fodder, and the economics of high-value overland cargo.
- A pack donkey's standard load of about two talents, the figure used across the Old Assyrian
  caravan archives at Kanesh. Those archives are several centuries earlier than this article's
  setting; the load is a fact about the animal rather than about the period, so it is used here.
- Caravan travel of roughly ten to fifteen kilometres a day over long distances.
- A working donkey's water requirement of roughly twenty litres a day in heat, its ability to
  go about three days without drinking, and its capacity to take on some thirty litres in a
  few minutes. These are modern veterinary figures for the same animal.
- Safe conduct in merchant correspondence as an arrangement of guest-obligation and named
  patronage rather than as purchased protection.
- Dried sour curd as a durable dairy technology, used here as an explicitly comparative reconstruction.
- Grain, onion, cumin, dried fruit, and oil as portable or locally purchased foods.
- The text treats wells as controlled stopping places where travellers paid for water and fodder. The particular village, family, and count by pebbles are invented.
- Load order, rubbed straps, watering sequence, and the night ledger derive from the practical constraints of pack travel rather than a surviving itinerary.

## Declared reconstruction

Mattan's nineteen years, his ownership of two of the six animals, the well-keeper and her
pricing, and every quantity attributed to this particular caravan are invented. The load,
water and pace figures behind Yadinu's arithmetic are real and are listed above.

The caravan, Mattan, collapsed donkey, route incidents, and meal are invented. The article derives the pot from the road’s constraints rather than claiming a surviving recipe.
